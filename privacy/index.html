3. What this policy covers

This policy explains what personal information Prano collects, why, who it is shared with, how long it is kept, and what rights apply to it. It covers the Prano mobile application, the website at https://pranoapp.com, and our support channels.

Read it alongside our Terms of Service, which is incorporated into this policy by reference for defined terms.

Washington, Nevada and Connecticut residents: consumer health data is also covered by our separate Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy.

4. Plain‑language summary

  • Prano works by looking at photos of barcodes, labels, meals and menus. Those photos, and what we work out from them, are the core of what we hold about you.
  • What we work out — a personalised health score, your dietary patterns, and the goals, allergies and self‑reported conditions you tell us about — is health‑related information. We treat it as sensitive because in several jurisdictions the law requires that, and because it is the honest description of what it is.
  • To analyse a scan and to answer a question in Ask Prano, we send the relevant image and/or text to Anthropic, our AI processor, from our own server — never directly from your device. Anthropic does not train its models on your content under our agreement with them.
  • We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it for cross‑context behavioural advertising. We do not use an advertising identifier and do not run an ad SDK.
  • You can delete any individual scan, and you can delete your entire account and all associated data, from inside the App — or from a web page even if you no longer have the App installed.
  • Prano is not a HIPAA‑covered service, and we do not claim to be. See §13.

7. Consent — what we ask for, and how to take it back

Prano's core function requires processing health‑related information about you. We ask for your express, opt‑in consent to that processing before any scan is analysed, separately from your acceptance of the Terms of Service, and we tell you at that point what we will collect and why.

8. Artificial intelligence — what actually happens to a scan

What happens when you scan. The image is transmitted over TLS to our own backend — a Supabase Edge Function — which forwards it to Anthropic's Claude API for analysis. Your device never talks to Anthropic directly; the call is always server‑side, from our infrastructure, under our account. The response is stored in our Supabase Postgres database and Supabase Storage, both hosted in the United States. The original image is retained until you delete the scan or your account (§11).

What happens when you use Ask Prano. Your question, together with the context of the relevant scan, is sent the same way — client to our Supabase Edge Function, then to Anthropic — and the response is returned and stored as part of your conversation history.

Barcode lookups. When you scan a barcode, our backend queries OpenFoodFacts, a public, crowd‑sourced product database, using the barcode value. This lookup sends only the barcode — not your account identity or profile.

Our commitments: - Anthropic acts as our processor, processing your content only on our documented instructions under a data processing agreement. - Your content is not used to train Anthropic's models. This is a contractual term, not a setting you have to find and toggle. - We do not sell, licence, or otherwise make your images or conversations available to any third party for that party's own purposes. - Where we use data to improve Prano's own scoring accuracy, we use aggregated or de‑identified data, unless you have separately and explicitly opted in to contributing identifiable scans.

Limits you should understand. AI systems make mistakes. A health score and the reasoning behind it are estimates. Ask Prano can be confidently wrong. Prano's output is general information about food — it is not personalised medical, dietary or clinical advice, and it must never be relied on for allergen safety. Always confirm allergens on the physical packaging.

13. HIPAA — why it does not apply, plainly

Prano is not a HIPAA‑covered entity, and it is not the business associate of one. The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act binds covered entities — healthcare providers who transmit health information electronically in connection with certain transactions, health plans, and healthcare clearinghouses — and their business associates, meaning organisations that create, receive, maintain or transmit protected health information on behalf of a covered entity. Prano is neither. It is a direct‑to‑consumer app: a person downloads it for themselves, outside any treatment relationship, and gives Prano information about themselves directly. That relationship sits outside HIPAA's scope entirely, no matter how health‑related the information is.

Prano is never described as "HIPAA compliant" in marketing, support responses, or the app itself, because it is not, and claiming it would itself be a deceptive‑practices risk.

What does bind Prano is listed in the sections around this one: the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule (§15), the FTC Act generally, Washington's My Health My Data Act and similar state laws (§14), CCPA/CPRA sensitive‑PI rules (§12.3), other state comprehensive privacy laws (§12.4), PIPEDA and Quebec Law 25, the Australian Privacy Act, and India's DPDP Act. Several of these are, in practical effect, more protective of a consumer's health data than HIPAA would be for this kind of app.

A separate Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy is published at its own URL, as Washington's My Health My Data Act requires. It describes the consumer health data Prano collects, why, who receives it, and your rights to withdraw consent to collection and, separately, to sharing.

In summary: the foods and products you scan, the nutritional composition and dietary patterns inferred from them, your dietary restrictions, allergies and intolerances, and self‑reported conditions are consumer health data. 15. The FTC Health Breach Notification Rule

The FTC's Health Breach Notification Rule, as amended in 2024, applies to vendors of personal health records and related entities, and its updated scope reaches consumer health apps like Prano that are not covered by HIPAA. Two things about it matter:

  • An unauthorised disclosure counts as a breach, not only unauthorised access — including through a vendor's security failure.
  • Notification timelines are short. Notice to affected individuals, the FTC and, for larger breaches, media, is required within 60 days of discovery.

If a breach affecting your personal information occurs, we will notify you and the relevant regulators as required under this Rule, the Australian Notifiable Data Breaches scheme, PIPEDA's breach‑reporting obligations, the DPDP Act, and applicable state breach‑notification statutes.

16. Security

We maintain administrative, technical and physical safeguards appropriate to the sensitivity of the data, including:

  • TLS encryption for all data in transit, including to Supabase and to Anthropic
  • Encryption at rest, provided by Supabase's underlying Postgres/storage infrastructure
  • Passwords stored only as salted, computationally expensive hashes — never in plain text (handled by Supabase Auth)
  • Row‑level security in the database, so one account cannot read another's data
  • Role‑based access control, with production data access limited to named personnel who require it
  • Multi‑factor authentication on administrative and infrastructure accounts
  • Audit logging of access to user data where the platform supports it
  • Regular dependency scanning and patching

No system is perfectly secure. You are responsible for keeping your account credentials confidential. Tell us immediately at contact@anantex.com if you believe your account has been compromised.
